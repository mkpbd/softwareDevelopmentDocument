# 1. CORE BUSINESS WORKFLOWS

---

## 🧑‍⚕️ 1A. PATIENT JOURNEY — OPD (Outpatient Department)

> **Think of it like this:** A patient visiting the hospital for a day visit — no overnight stay. Like going to a shop, getting what you need, and going home.

---

### STEP 1: Patient Registration

- A new patient walks in → Front Desk checks if they already exist in the system (search by phone number, national ID, or name)
- **If existing patient:** Pull up their profile → confirm identity → proceed
- **If new patient:** Create a new Patient Record capturing:
    - Full name, date of birth, gender, blood group
    - Contact number, emergency contact
    - National ID / Passport number
    - Address
    - Assign a **unique Patient ID (MRN — Medical Record Number)** that stays with them for life
- Patient gets a **registration card/token** (physical or digital via SMS)

**Business Rule:** Every patient — regardless of ability to pay — must be registered before any service is rendered (except life-threatening emergencies handled in ER).

---

### STEP 2: Appointment Booking

- Patient selects department (e.g., Cardiology) and preferred doctor
- System shows available slots based on doctor's schedule
- Appointment is booked → Patient gets confirmation (SMS/WhatsApp/Portal notification)
- Appointment type is set: **Walk-in / Scheduled / Teleconsultation / Referred**
- If patient is referred from another doctor/hospital → link referral source to the record

**Business Rule:** One patient cannot have two appointments with the same doctor at the same time slot. The system auto-blocks the slot upon booking.

---

### STEP 3: Arrival & Check-In

- Patient arrives at the hospital → goes to OPD reception
- Reception staff marks the patient as **"Arrived"** in the system
- Patient is assigned a **Queue Number** for the specific doctor's chamber
- Queue Management System displays the token on waiting room screens
- Patient vitals may be taken by a nurse before the doctor sees them:
    - Blood Pressure, Temperature, Pulse, Weight, Height, SpO2
    - Vitals are recorded and automatically attached to today's visit record (EMR)

---

### STEP 4: Consultation (Doctor's Workflow)

- Doctor's screen shows the queue — calls the next patient
- Doctor reviews:
    - Patient's **past medical history** (all previous visits, diagnoses, allergies, medications)
    - Today's vitals entered by nurse
    - Any previous lab or radiology results
- Doctor performs consultation and records in **Electronic Medical Record (EMR)**:
    - Chief Complaint (why the patient came)
    - Clinical Findings / Examination Notes
    - Diagnosis (using ICD-10/ICD-11 codes for standardization)
    - Treatment Plan
- Doctor issues **Prescription** (digitally):
    - Medicines (name, dosage, frequency, duration)
    - Lab Tests ordered (e.g., CBC, Blood Sugar)
    - Radiology ordered (e.g., X-Ray, Ultrasound)
    - Follow-up date if needed
- Doctor marks consultation as **"Completed"**

**Business Rule:** Doctor can only prescribe medicines that are not contraindicated with the patient's known allergies. CDSS (Clinical Decision Support System) raises an automatic alert if a conflict is detected.

---

### STEP 5: Lab / Radiology Execution (if ordered)

- Prescription with lab/radiology orders is visible instantly in the Lab and Radiology departments
    
- **Lab Workflow:**
    
    - Lab technician sees the pending test order → collects sample → marks sample collected
    - Test is run → results entered → verified by Lab Supervisor
    - Results automatically appear in the patient's EMR
    - Patient/Doctor is notified (SMS/Portal)
- **Radiology Workflow:**
    
    - Radiology technician sees the imaging order
    - Patient is called for imaging (X-Ray, CT Scan, MRI, etc.)
    - Images captured → Radiologist reviews and writes a **report**
    - Report + images uploaded to EMR
    - Doctor is notified

**Business Rule:** No lab test or radiology imaging can be performed without a valid doctor's order linked to a patient visit. Walk-up requests must be approved by a doctor.

---

### STEP 6: Pharmacy Dispensing (if medicine prescribed)

- Pharmacist sees the digital prescription → verifies:
    - Medicine is in stock
    - Dosage is within safe limits (CDSS check)
    - Patient has no allergy to the medicine
- Pharmacist dispenses medicines → marks prescription as **"Dispensed"**
- Inventory is automatically deducted by the exact dispensed quantity
- If a medicine is out of stock → Pharmacist suggests a substitute or notifies patient/doctor

**Business Rule:** Pharmacist cannot dispense more than the prescribed quantity in a single visit for controlled substances (narcotics, psychotropics). These require special logging.

---

### STEP 7: OPD Billing & Payment

- All services rendered in this visit are automatically pulled into the billing screen:
    - Consultation fee
    - Lab test charges
    - Radiology charges
    - Medicine charges (if dispensed from hospital pharmacy)
- Billing Officer reviews the bill → applies any applicable discounts (employee, loyalty, package)
- **Payment Methods accepted:** Cash, Card, Mobile Banking (bKash/Nagad), Insurance, Corporate Credit
- **If Insurance:** Verify eligibility → Submit claim details → Get pre-authorization if required → Patient pays co-pay if applicable
- Receipt is generated → visit is financially closed

---

### STEP 8: Visit Closure & Follow-Up

- Patient departs → visit status marked **"Completed"**
- Patient receives SMS summary: diagnosis, medicines, follow-up date
- Patient can view full visit summary on the **Patient Portal**
- Follow-up appointment (if set) appears in the scheduling calendar

---

## 🏥 1B. PATIENT JOURNEY — IPD (Inpatient Department)

> **Think of it like this:** The patient needs to stay in the hospital overnight (or multiple nights). This is much more complex — think of it like checking into a hotel, but with doctors, nurses, medicines, and operations happening every day.

---

### STEP 1: Admission Decision

- Patient can be admitted through three pathways:
    
    1. **From OPD:** Doctor in OPD decides the patient needs admission → issues an Admission Order
    2. **From Emergency (ER):** Emergency doctor stabilizes patient → decides to admit to a ward
    3. **Pre-planned Admission:** Patient comes with a scheduled surgery/procedure date
- Admission Order contains:
    
    - Admitting Doctor's name
    - Department/Ward (e.g., Cardiology, Surgery, Orthopedics)
    - Bed type requested (General, Semi-Private, Private, ICU, HDU)
    - Reason for admission / provisional diagnosis
    - Patient condition (Stable, Serious, Critical)

---

### STEP 2: Bed Assignment

- Bed Management team receives the admission request
- System shows **real-time bed availability** by ward, floor, and bed type
- Bed is assigned → status changes: **Available → Reserved → Occupied**
- Patient and attendant are guided to the assigned room/bed

**Business Rule:** ICU beds can only be assigned by senior doctors (Consultant or above). General ward beds can be assigned by admission staff.

---

### STEP 3: Admission Processing

- Admission desk creates a formal **IPD Admission Record**:
    - Admission date and time
    - Admitted by which doctor
    - Patient's current condition and chief complaint
    - Next-of-kin / Guardian information
    - Insurance details (if applicable) — pre-authorization initiated immediately
- An **Advance Deposit** is collected (mandatory for non-emergency cases)
    - Advance amount is defined by expected treatment type (e.g., Surgery = BDT 50,000 advance)
    - This acts as a security deposit — deducted from final bill at discharge
- Patient is issued an **IPD Wristband** with name, MRN, bed number, blood group (safety tool)

**Business Rule:** If advance cannot be paid due to financial hardship, the case is escalated to the **Social Welfare Officer** who can authorize a reduced advance or deferred payment plan.

---

### STEP 4: Daily Clinical Care (Nursing & Doctor Rounds)

> This happens every single day of the hospital stay. Think of it as the daily engine of inpatient care.

**Doctor's Duty:**

- Attending doctor visits the patient daily (Doctor's Round)
- Records **Progress Notes** in EMR: How is the patient today? Better? Worse?
- Updates or adds new orders:
    - Medication orders (IV drips, injections, tablets)
    - Lab tests (daily blood work, cultures, etc.)
    - Radiology (CT scan, repeat X-Ray)
    - Diet orders (liquid diet, diabetic diet, nil by mouth before surgery)
    - Specialist consultation requests (e.g., calling a Nephrologist for kidney issues)
    - Nursing instructions (patient positioning, wound care frequency)

**Nurse's Duty (Nursing Workflow):**

- Nurse receives all doctor orders on their nursing station screen
- **Medication Administration:**
    - Nurse requests medicine from Pharmacy (if not at nursing station)
    - Pharmacy dispenses → Nurse receives and records
    - Nurse administers medicine at the correct time → scans patient wristband (barcode/RFID) to confirm correct patient
    - Records administration in **Medication Administration Record (MAR)**:
        - Drug name, dose, route (IV/Oral), time, nurse's name
- **Vitals Monitoring:** Recorded every shift (or more frequently in ICU)
- **Nursing Notes:** Any observations, patient complaints, incidents recorded
- **Shift Handover:** At end of each shift, outgoing nurse documents patient status for incoming nurse

**Business Rule:** The 5 Rights of Medication Administration are enforced by the system:

1. Right Patient (wristband scan)
2. Right Medicine (matches prescription)
3. Right Dose (matches prescription)
4. Right Route (matches prescription)
5. Right Time (within the scheduled time window)

---

### STEP 5: Procedures, Surgeries, & Special Services

- If patient requires surgery:
    - **Surgeon creates an Operation Theater (OT) Request**
    - OT Coordinator schedules the procedure → assigns OT room, date, time, surgical team
    - Pre-operative checklist completed (blood group confirmed, allergies noted, consents signed)
    - Pre-anesthesia assessment done by Anesthesiologist
    - Surgery performed → OT notes recorded (procedure, implants used, materials consumed)
    - All materials used in surgery (sutures, implants, surgical equipment) are automatically charged to patient's bill
    - Post-operative patient moved to Recovery Room → then back to ward or ICU

---

### STEP 6: IPD Billing (Running Bill)

- A running bill accumulates throughout the patient's stay — every service adds to it automatically:
    - Daily bed charges (per night)
    - Doctor visit charges (each round)
    - Nursing charges
    - All medicines administered
    - Lab tests
    - Radiology tests
    - OT charges (surgeon, anesthesiologist, OT room)
    - Implant/material charges
    - Specialist consultation fees
    - Diet charges
    - Miscellaneous services (physiotherapy, dialysis, etc.)
- Patient/attendant can request an **Interim Bill** at any time to see the running total
- **Advance deposit is tracked** — system alerts billing when balance is running low → billing team requests additional advance

---

### STEP 7: Discharge Planning

- Treating Doctor decides the patient is ready to go home → issues **Discharge Order**
- Discharge Order triggers multiple parallel actions:
    1. **Nursing:** Prepares patient, removes IV lines, gives discharge instructions
    2. **Pharmacy:** Final medicine dispensing for home use (discharge medicines)
    3. **Billing:** Finalizes the complete bill

**Discharge Summary (EMR):**

- Doctor prepares a formal Discharge Summary containing:
    - Admission and discharge dates
    - Final diagnosis
    - Procedures performed
    - All medicines given during stay
    - Condition at discharge
    - Medicines to take at home
    - Follow-up appointment
    - Any dietary/activity restrictions
- Discharge summary is printed and given to patient + stored in EMR

---

### STEP 8: Final Billing & Payment at Discharge

- Final bill is generated — advance deposit is deducted
- **If Insurance:** Submit complete claim with all supporting documents (discharge summary, lab reports, prescriptions, itemized bill) → Insurance company reviews → approves/partially approves/rejects
- Patient pays remaining balance (or receives refund if advance > total bill)
- **If patient cannot pay:** Escalate to Hospital Administrator → negotiate payment plan
- Receipt issued → **Discharge Clearance Note** generated
- Bed status changed: **Occupied → Cleaning** → Housekeeping is notified

**Business Rule (Hard Stop):** Patient CANNOT receive the official Discharge Summary and leave the hospital if their bill has an unpaid balance AND there is no approved insurance claim, approved payment plan, or authorized waiver in the system. This is enforced at the system level.

---

### STEP 9: Post-Discharge Follow-Up

- System auto-schedules follow-up appointment (if doctor specified)
- CRM system flags patient for a **courtesy call** within 48 hours (How are you feeling? Any issues?)
- Patient can access their complete discharge summary on the Patient Portal
- If patient is re-admitted within 30 days for the same diagnosis → system flags it as a **Readmission** (important quality metric)

---

## 🛒 1C. PROCUREMENT CYCLE

> **Think of it like this:** The hospital needs to buy medicines, surgical gloves, or an X-Ray machine. This is the full journey from "we need it" to "it's in our stockroom."

---

### STEP 1: Need Identification (Purchase Requisition)

- **Trigger 1 — Automatic:** Inventory system detects that a medicine/item has reached its **Reorder Level** (e.g., Paracetamol stock = 50 units, Reorder Level = 200 units → system auto-generates a Purchase Requisition)
- **Trigger 2 — Manual:** Department head (e.g., Head Nurse, Lab Manager) identifies a need and submits a manual Purchase Requisition
- Purchase Requisition contains:
    - Item name and specification
    - Quantity required
    - Required by date
    - Requesting department
    - Justification (for new or non-standard items)

---

### STEP 2: Approval Workflow

- Requisition enters an approval chain based on value:
    - **Up to BDT 10,000:** Department Head approves
    - **BDT 10,001 – 1,00,000:** Finance Manager + Department Head approve
    - **Above BDT 1,00,000:** CEO/CFO approval required
    - **Capital Equipment (above BDT 5,00,000):** Board-level approval + tendering process

**Business Rule:** No Purchase Order can be issued without completing the full approval chain. The system blocks PO creation if approvals are pending.

---

### STEP 3: Vendor Selection & Quotation

- Purchasing team sends **Request for Quotation (RFQ)** to shortlisted vendors
- Vendors submit their quotes (price, delivery timeline, warranty, payment terms)
- If order value is above threshold → **Tendering process** is mandatory:
    - Vendors submit sealed bids
    - Purchase Committee evaluates on price, quality, delivery, vendor reputation
- Preferred vendor selected → **Purchase Order (PO)** is created and issued

**Business Rule:** For medicines, vendor must have a valid **Drug License** registered in the system. For medical equipment, vendor must provide CE/FDA/BMDA certification. System blocks PO creation if vendor certifications are expired.

---

### STEP 4: Goods Receipt & Quality Check

- Goods arrive at the hospital store → **Goods Receipt Note (GRN)** is created
- Store Keeper checks:
    - Quantity matches PO
    - Item matches specification
    - For medicines: Batch number, Manufacturing date, Expiry date (minimum 6 months remaining required)
    - Physical condition (no damage, proper packaging)
- **Partial Receipt:** If only part of the order arrives → record partial GRN → PO remains open for the balance
- If quality check fails → goods are rejected → vendor notified → Return/Replacement requested

**Business Rule:** No medicine can be accepted into inventory if its expiry date is less than 6 months away. The system enforces this and prevents GRN completion for such items.

---

### STEP 5: Inventory Update & Invoice Processing

- After GRN approval → inventory is automatically updated (stock increases)
- Items are tagged with:
    - Batch number (for medicines)
    - Asset tag number (for equipment)
    - Location (which store/shelf)
- Vendor's Invoice is matched against:
    - Purchase Order (price, quantity)
    - GRN (what was actually received)
- This is the **3-Way Match:** PO + GRN + Invoice must match for payment to proceed
- If they match → Invoice is approved → forwarded to Accounts Payable

---

### STEP 6: Payment to Vendor

- Accounts Payable team processes payment as per agreed credit terms (e.g., Net 30 days)
- Payment is made via bank transfer / cheque
- Payment record is linked to the Invoice and PO → transaction closed
- Vendor account in the system is updated → ledger entry posted

---

## 💰 1D. REVENUE CYCLE MANAGEMENT (RCM)

> **Think of it like this:** The hospital provides a service → but how does it actually get paid? RCM is the entire journey from "service rendered" to "money in the bank."

---

### STEP 1: Patient Registration & Insurance Verification

- At registration → patient's insurance card details are captured
- System performs **real-time eligibility check** with the insurance company:
    - Is the patient covered?
    - What services are covered?
    - What is the co-pay / deductible?
    - Has the deductible been met?
- **Pre-Authorization (Prior Auth):**
    - For expensive procedures (surgery, CT scan, ICU admission), insurance company's pre-authorization is required BEFORE the service
    - Hospital submits pre-auth request → insurance approves/rejects/requests more info
    - If approved → auth number recorded → service can proceed

---

### STEP 2: Service Delivery & Charge Capture

- Every single service performed is recorded against the patient's account in real-time
- Each service has a predefined **Charge Code** with a standard price
- Services are also coded using:
    - **ICD codes** (diagnosis codes — what the patient has)
    - **CPT/procedure codes** (what was done to the patient)
- These codes are critical for insurance claims — wrong codes = rejected claim

**Business Rule:** Every chargeable service must have at minimum one valid ICD code linked to it. System prevents bill finalization if any service lacks proper coding.

---

### STEP 3: Bill Generation & Review

- At discharge (IPD) or visit end (OPD) → complete bill is generated
- Billing Officer reviews:
    - Are all services captured?
    - Are any duplicate charges present?
    - Are the insurance codes correct?
    - Has the advance deposit been accounted for?
- Apply approved discounts, package rates, or waivers
- **Insurance portion** is separated from **Patient portion**

---

### STEP 4: Insurance Claim Submission

- Claim package prepared:
    - Patient demographics
    - Insurance ID / policy number
    - Diagnosis codes (ICD)
    - Procedure codes (CPT)
    - Itemized bill
    - Supporting documents (lab reports, discharge summary, doctor's notes)
    - Pre-authorization number
- Claim submitted to insurance company (electronically or physically)
- Claim is assigned a **Tracking Number** and status changes to **"Submitted"**

---

### STEP 5: Claim Follow-Up & Adjudication

- Insurance company reviews the claim (this can take days to weeks)
    
- Possible outcomes:
    
    - **Approved:** Full payment as per policy → payment issued to hospital
    - **Partially Approved:** Some items approved, others not → partial payment
    - **Pending Information:** Insurance needs more documents → hospital submits and resubmits
    - **Rejected:** Claim denied (invalid code, not covered, pre-auth missing, etc.)
- **For Rejections:**
    
    - Billing team reviews the rejection reason
    - If correctable (wrong code, missing document) → correct and **resubmit within appeal window**
    - If non-correctable → patient is billed for the rejected amount

---

### STEP 6: Payment Posting & Reconciliation

- Insurance payment received → posted against the specific claim
    
- **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** document from insurance is matched with the payment
    
- Any difference between what was billed and what was paid is classified as:
    
    - **Contractual Adjustment:** Pre-agreed discount with the insurance company (normal, written off)
    - **Patient Responsibility:** Co-pay, deductible, non-covered services (billed to patient)
    - **Denial:** Will be appealed or written off
- Patient's remaining balance is:
    
    - Collected at discharge
    - Or sent as a statement with a payment due date
    - Or handed over to **Collections** if unpaid after a defined period

---

### STEP 7: Accounts Receivable (AR) Management

- All outstanding amounts are tracked in the AR module
- AR is aged into buckets:
    - **0-30 days** (current)
    - **31-60 days** (follow up)
    - **61-90 days** (escalate)
    - **90+ days** (collections or write-off consideration)
- AR team makes regular follow-up calls/messages for unpaid balances
- **Write-offs** require authorization from Finance Manager or CFO

## 🔗 SCENARIO A: Nurse Administers Medicine to an IPD Patient

Here's what happens across FIVE modules simultaneously:

text

```
DOCTOR (EMR Module)
  → Writes medication order: "Amoxicillin 500mg IV every 8 hours"
      ↓
PHARMACY MODULE
  → Receives the order → Pharmacist verifies → Dispenses medicine to the ward
  → Checks INVENTORY: Deducts 3 units of Amoxicillin 500mg from IPD Ward stock
      ↓
NURSING MODULE
  → Nurse receives dispensed medicine → Scans patient wristband (correct patient check)
  → Administers medicine → Records in MAR (Medication Administration Record)
  → Time, dose, route, and nurse ID are all logged
      ↓
IPD BILLING MODULE
  → Amoxicillin 500mg x 3 units automatically added to patient's running bill
  → Price pulled from standard charge master
      ↓
INVENTORY MODULE
  → Stock level updated in real-time
  → If stock reaches Reorder Level → Auto-generates Purchase Requisition
  → PROCUREMENT MODULE is triggered for restocking
```

# 3. KEY BUSINESS RULES & VALIDATIONS

> **Think of it like this:** These are the unbreakable laws of the hospital system. Like traffic lights — you MUST follow them. The system enforces them automatically.

---

### 🔴 RULE 1: Discharge Block — No Money, No Discharge Paper

**Rule:** A patient cannot receive their official Discharge Summary (the document they need to leave) if:

- Their IPD bill has an outstanding balance AND
- There is no approved insurance coverage for the balance AND
- No authorized payment plan or waiver exists

**Why:** This is the hospital's financial safeguard. The system literally grays out the "Complete Discharge" button until one of the payment conditions is met.

**Exception:** The patient's physical discharge (they can walk out) is a clinical right — but the Discharge Summary document is withheld until financial clearance. This is the ethical and legal middle ground.

---

### 🔴 RULE 2: Allergy Alert — Hard Stop on Dangerous Prescriptions

**Rule:** When a doctor tries to prescribe a medicine that is listed in the patient's known allergy profile:

- **Yellow Alert:** The CDSS shows a warning but allows the doctor to override with a documented reason
- **Red Alert (Severe Allergy):** The system creates a hard stop — the prescription CANNOT be saved until the doctor documents a clinical justification AND a second doctor countersigns

**Why:** Patient safety. This prevents life-threatening allergic reactions.

---

### 🔴 RULE 3: Blood Bank — Cross-Match Before Release

**Rule:** No unit of blood can be released from the Blood Bank to a patient unless:

- The patient's blood type is confirmed (typed and screened)
- A cross-match test is performed between the donor blood and the patient's blood
- The cross-match result is recorded as "Compatible" in the system

**Exception:** Life-threatening emergency → "O Negative" (universal donor) blood can be released before cross-match with a mandatory emergency override reason documented.

---

### 🔴 RULE 4: Medicine Expiry — Zero Tolerance

**Rule:** The system checks expiry dates at two critical points:

1. **At receiving (GRN):** Medicines with less than 6 months expiry cannot be accepted
2. **At dispensing:** Any medicine whose expiry date has passed TODAY or is in the past cannot be dispensed. The system auto-blocks the dispensing action.

**Proactive:** The system generates a daily "Near-Expiry Report" (medicines expiring in 30, 60, 90 days) so pharmacy can use them first or return to vendor.

---

### 🔴 RULE 5: Bed Assignment — ICU Requires Senior Authorization

**Rule:** ICU and HDU (High Dependency Unit) beds can only be assigned/confirmed by a doctor with the rank of **Consultant or above**. A junior doctor or nurse cannot independently assign an ICU bed.

**Why:** ICU is a resource-scarce, high-cost environment. Clinical judgment at a senior level ensures the right patients are in ICU.

---

### 🔴 RULE 6: Controlled Substance Dispensing — Dual Validation

**Rule:** Narcotics, opioids, and psychotropic medicines require:

- A physical prescription (even if entered digitally)
- TWO pharmacist signatures for dispensing
- Quantity dispensed cannot exceed the prescribed amount by even 1 unit
- A **Controlled Drug Register** entry with patient name, MRN, drug, dose, quantity, date, and pharmacist name

**Why:** Legal compliance. Controlled substance abuse and diversion is a serious criminal and ethical issue.

---

### 🔴 RULE 7: Purchase Order — 3-Way Match is Mandatory for Payment

**Rule:** An invoice from a vendor CANNOT be approved for payment unless the system confirms:

- The Invoice matches the Purchase Order (price, quantity, items)
- The Invoice matches the Goods Receipt Note (what was actually received)
- All three documents are linked in the system

**Why:** Prevents fraud. Prevents paying for goods not received. Prevents overpayment.

---

### 🔴 RULE 8: Doctor Cannot Prescribe Without Active Registration

**Rule:** A doctor's profile must have:

- Valid Medical Council Registration number (BMDC in Bangladesh)
- Registration expiry date must be in the future
- If registration is expired → system blocks the doctor from writing prescriptions and shows an alert to admin

**Why:** Legal compliance. Prescriptions from unregistered doctors are illegal.

---

### 🔴 RULE 9: Insurance Pre-Authorization Before High-Cost Services

**Rule:** For any service above a defined cost threshold (or for specific service types defined by insurance contract — e.g., surgeries, CT scans, ICU admission):

- Pre-authorization from the insurance company must be obtained and recorded in the system BEFORE the service is performed
- Without an auth number → the service can still be performed (clinical need comes first) but it is flagged as "Pre-Auth Missing" → significantly higher risk of claim rejection → billing team is alerted immediately

---

### 🔴 RULE 10: Advance Deposit — Mandatory for Non-Emergency IPD Admissions

**Rule:** For planned IPD admissions:

- Minimum advance deposit must be collected before the patient is shown to their bed
- Advance amount is determined by expected treatment category (e.g., Normal Delivery: BDT 15,000, Cardiac Surgery: BDT 1,50,000)
- Exception requires Finance Manager authorization
- System tracks advance balance vs. running bill — alerts billing when running bill exceeds 80% of advance collected

---

### 🔴 RULE 11: One Active Admission Per Patient

**Rule:** A patient cannot have two simultaneous active IPD admissions in the same hospital (except authorized transfer scenarios). The system detects the active admission and blocks a duplicate admission — requires admin override with reason.

---

### 🔴 RULE 12: Payroll Cannot Be Run Without Attendance Lock

**Rule:** Payroll for a month can only be processed after the attendance for that month is "Locked" by the HR Manager. Once locked:

- No attendance changes can be made
- Payroll calculation runs on the locked data
- This prevents retroactive tampering

---

### 🔴 RULE 13: Lab Results — Verified Before EMR Visibility

**Rule:** Lab results entered by a technician are in **"Pending Verification"** status. They are NOT visible to the doctor or patient until a Lab Supervisor or Senior Technician verifies them.

**Critical Value Rule:** If a result falls into a "Critical Value" range (e.g., potassium > 6.5 mEq/L — life-threatening), the system immediately sends a **Critical Alert** to the ordering doctor's screen and phone, requiring the doctor to acknowledge receipt.

---

### 🔴 RULE 14: Role-Based Access — Minimum Necessary Information

**Rule:** Every user can only see the information they need to do their job.

- A billing officer can see financial data but CANNOT see clinical notes
- A nurse can see medication orders but CANNOT modify the bill
- A pharmacist can see prescriptions but CANNOT view financial account details
- Violation attempts are logged in the Audit Trail

---

### 🔴 RULE 15: Vendor Blacklisting

**Rule:** A vendor can be blacklisted in the system for:

- Supplying counterfeit or substandard medicines
- Consistent delivery failures
- Fraud/corruption  
    **Effect:** A blacklisted vendor CANNOT receive any new Purchase Order. System blocks PO creation for blacklisted vendors. Blacklisting requires CFO/CEO authorization and is permanently logged in the Audit Trail.

---

# 4. USER ROLES & OPERATIONAL RESPONSIBILITIES

> **Think of it like this:** In a hospital, everyone has a specific job. Like in a restaurant — the chef cooks, the waiter serves, the cashier takes money. No one else touches the cash register.

---

## 👥 CORE CLINICAL ROLES

### 🩺 Attending Physician / Doctor

**Can Do:**

- View patient's complete medical history and EMR
- Write clinical notes, progress notes, diagnosis
- Write prescriptions (medicines, lab tests, radiology)
- Issue admission orders and discharge orders
- Request specialist consultations
- View lab and radiology results
- Order diet and nursing care instructions
- Issue OT (surgery) requests

**Cannot Do:**

- Modify billing or financial records
- Change pharmacy stock levels
- Approve vendor invoices
- Access other patients' records without authorization (system logs the access)

---

### 👩‍⚕️ Head Nurse / Senior Nurse

**Can Do:**

- View and acknowledge all doctor orders for their ward
- Record vitals, nursing notes, patient observations
- Administer medications and record in MAR
- Perform patient assessments
- Assign nurses to patients for each shift
- Conduct and document shift handovers
- Request medicines from pharmacy for the ward
- Raise housekeeping requests (bed cleaning after discharge)
- Flag patient deterioration (escalation alerts)

**Cannot Do:**

- Write prescriptions (this is exclusively a doctor's role)
- Modify lab results
- Approve financial transactions

---

### 💊 Chief Pharmacist / Pharmacist

**Can Do:**

- View all active prescriptions (OPD and IPD)
- Verify and dispense medicines against valid prescriptions
- Perform drug interaction and allergy checks
- Manage pharmacy inventory (receive stock, record dispensing)

**Cannot Do:**

- Write or modify prescriptions

### 🔬 Lab Technician / Radiologist

- View imaging orders

- Write and sign radiology reports

### 🩸 Blood Bank Officer

- Process advance deposits and refunds
- View patient's financial account history

- Write off amounts above their authorization limit (escalates to Finance Manager)

---

### 🛒 Purchase Officer / Procurement Manager

- Assign roles and permissions
- Configure system settings and master data
- View audit trails

- Set up integration with third-party systems

# 6. EDGE CASES & EXCEPTION HANDLING

> **Think of it like this:** These are the "what if something goes wrong?" scenarios. Every great hospital system must have a plan for the unusual situations.

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 1: Emergency Patient Arrives Without Money or ID

**Scenario:** An unconscious patient is brought in by strangers. No ID, no money, no insurance card.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** Triage immediately classifies as P1 (Critical) — treatment begins WITHOUT any financial formalities
- **Step 2:** Minimal emergency registration is created with what's known:
    - "Unknown Patient" or the name given by accompanying person
    - Approximate age/gender from physical appearance
    - A temporary MRN is assigned ("UNKNOWN-20250615-001")
- **Step 3:** All ER services are rendered and charged to the temporary account
- **Step 4:** Simultaneously, staff attempt to identify the patient:
    - Check pockets/belongings (respectfully) for any ID
    - Ask companions
    - Check if face matches any existing patient record
- **Step 5:** Once stable → Social Welfare Officer is notified
    - Social Welfare assesses eligibility for **Charitable Care / Government Scheme / Hospital Welfare Fund**
- **Step 6:** If patient is identified later → their temporary record is merged with their real MRN
- **Step 7:** If patient is unable to pay after recovery → case goes through:
    - Payment plan setup
    - Partial waiver with Finance Manager authorization
    - Charitable write-off with CEO/Board authorization
    - Referral to government welfare program

**Key Principle:** NO clinical care is withheld due to financial inability in an emergency. The financial issue is resolved AFTER stabilization.

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 2: Insurance Claim is Rejected After Patient Has Been Discharged

**Scenario:** Patient was discharged, insurance was supposed to pay BDT 80,000. Insurance rejects the claim 3 weeks later.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** System immediately flags the claim as "Rejected" → Billing team notified
- **Step 2:** Billing team analyzes the rejection reason code:
    - **"Wrong ICD code"** → Correct the code → Resubmit (if within appeal window — usually 30-90 days)
    - **"Service not covered under policy"** → Patient must be billed
    - **"Pre-authorization missing"** → Hospital's internal failure → Raise an appeal with documentation; if fails, internal accountability review
    - **"Policy was inactive on date of service"** → Patient must be billed
- **Step 3:** If the claim can be appealed → formal appeal letter submitted to insurance with supporting clinical evidence
- **Step 4:** If appeal also rejected → the amount becomes the patient's responsibility:
    - Patient is contacted → Statement of Balance sent
    - Payment plan offered (installments)
    - If patient disputes → Billing Manager reviews
- **Step 5:** If non-recoverable (patient can't pay, appeal failed) → Finance Manager authorizes write-off → classified as "Bad Debt" in GL
- **Step 6:** Root cause analysis done internally → What went wrong? Was pre-auth missed? Wrong code? → Process improvement action

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 3: A Prescribed Medicine Expires Today

**Scenario:** A nurse goes to dispense a medicine to an IPD patient. The system shows the medicine expires TODAY.

**Business Handling:**

- **Rule:** "Expires Today" = Expired. The system blocks dispensing immediately.
- **Step 1:** System generates an alert: "Medicine XYZ Batch #001 expires on [today's date]. Cannot dispense."
- **Step 2:** Pharmacist is notified → Checks if any other batch of the same medicine with a future expiry is available
    - **If yes:** Dispense from the valid batch — no disruption to patient care
    - **If no:** Pharmacist marks the medicine as "Out of Stock (Expiry)" → Initiates emergency procurement
- **Step 3:** Pharmacist informs the nurse and doctor → Doctor decides:
    - **Suitable substitute exists?** → Doctor amends prescription to substitute → Pharmacist dispenses substitute
    - **No substitute?** → Emergency purchase from nearest licensed supplier (local pharmacy purchase if needed, with proper documentation)
- **Step 4:** Expired stock is physically segregated → labeled "EXPIRED — DO NOT USE"
- **Step 5:** Expired medicines are disposed of per **pharmaceutical waste disposal regulations** (not thrown in regular trash — this is a legal requirement)
- **Step 6:** Inventory system updated → expired batch written off → loss recorded in GL as "Expired Medicine Write-Off"
- **Step 7:** Root cause reviewed: Why was medicine accepted with near-expiry date? Was the 6-month receiving rule followed? Accountability review.

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 4: Doctor Prescribes a Medicine That is Out of Stock

**Scenario:** OPD doctor prescribes Metformin 500mg for a diabetic patient, but pharmacy has zero stock.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** As soon as doctor finalizes the prescription, the CDSS checks real-time pharmacy stock
- **Step 2:** If out of stock → Doctor sees an orange warning: "Metformin 500mg is currently out of stock in pharmacy"
- **Step 3:** Doctor's options:
    - **Substitute:** System suggests bioequivalent alternatives (same drug class, similar effectiveness) → Doctor selects substitute → Prescription updated
    - **Keep Original:** Doctor can still prescribe it → Patient is informed it's unavailable at hospital pharmacy → Patient can get it from an external pharmacy → Prescription printed/sent digitally
- **Step 4:** Pharmacy auto-generates a Purchase Requisition for out-of-stock item (if it meets reorder criteria)
- **Step 5:** If patient is an IPD patient and this is a critical medicine → Pharmacist can raise an **Emergency Purchase Request** for immediate procurement from a local supplier

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 5: A Nurse Administers the Wrong Medicine (Medication Error)

**Scenario:** Nurse accidentally gives Patient A's medicine to Patient B (wrong patient) or gives the wrong dose.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** The wristband scanning system should have PREVENTED this:
    - System would have shown a mismatch alert when nurse scanned Patient B's wristband for Patient A's medicine
    - Nurse should not have overridden this alert
- **If it still happens (override or system failure):**
- **Step 2:** Nurse immediately reports to Head Nurse and Duty Doctor → Patient is assessed for any adverse reaction
- **Step 3:** An **Incident Report** is formally raised in the system (mandatory, non-negotiable):
    - What happened, when, who was involved, what medicine, what dose
    - This is confidential and used for quality improvement, NOT primarily for punishment
- **Step 4:** Clinical team monitors patient closely → any treatment needed for adverse reaction is provided
- **Step 5:** The event is recorded in the patient's EMR as a clinical note
- **Step 6:** Incident goes to **Patient Safety Committee** for review
- **Step 7:** Root cause analysis → Was it a system failure? Human error? Training gap? → Corrective action taken

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 6: Power Failure / System Downtime During Critical Operations

**Scenario:** The hospital ERP system goes down during an active surgery or during OPD hours.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** All critical workstations have **Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)** to allow graceful system shutdown
- **Step 2:** Hospital switches to **Downtime Procedures (Manual/Paper-Based Backup):**
    - Pre-printed prescription pads, lab request forms, and paper MAR sheets are always available
    - Nurses continue care using paper documentation
    - Pharmacy uses paper prescription slips
    - Registration desk uses pre-registered forms
- **Step 3:** All paper records are stamped with date/time and "Downtime Period"
- **Step 4:** Once system is restored → all downtime activities are entered into the system:
    - Lab tests ordered, medicines dispensed, vitals recorded
    - A "Downtime Entry" flag is attached to these records for auditability
- **Step 5:** IT team investigates root cause → Recovery Time Objective (RTO) is defined (e.g., system must be back within 2 hours)
- **Step 6:** System has daily automated backups + real-time replication to a secondary server (disaster recovery)

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 7: A Patient Tests Positive for a Notifiable Infectious Disease (e.g., Cholera, Ebola)

**Scenario:** Lab results confirm a patient has a legally reportable infectious disease.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** Lab system automatically flags the result as "Notifiable Disease"
- **Step 2:** Immediate Critical Alert sent to:
    - Treating Doctor
    - Infection Control Officer
    - Hospital Medical Director
- **Step 3:** Infection Control Protocol activated:
    - Patient placed in isolation
    - Contact tracing initiated (who has the patient been near in the hospital?)
    - PPE protocol for all staff in contact
- **Step 4:** Mandatory government notification:
    - Report filed with the local Health Authority (e.g., DGDA in Bangladesh, Ministry of Health) within the legally required timeframe
    - System generates the standard notification form pre-filled with patient (anonymized in some cases) and disease data
- **Step 5:** All contacts in the hospital who may have been exposed are documented and monitored

---

## 🚨 EDGE CASE 8: Duplicate Patient Records (Same Person Registered Twice)

**Scenario:** A patient was registered 2 years ago but registered again today because they forgot or lost their card — now they have two MRNs with split medical history.

**Business Handling:**

- **Step 1:** System's duplicate detection runs at registration:
    - Matching on: Name + Date of Birth + Phone Number + National ID
    - If similarity score is above a threshold → System warns: "Possible duplicate patient found" → Shows potential matches
- **Step 2:** Front Desk compares and confirms if it is indeed the same person
- **Step 3:** If duplicate confirmed → **Patient Merge** process initiated:
    - One MRN is designated as the "Master" (usually the older one)
    - All records from the "Duplicate" MRN (visits, prescriptions, bills) are merged into the Master
    - Duplicate MRN is retired (not deleted — kept for audit trail)
- **Step 4:** Merge requires approval from a supervisor and is permanently logged in the audit trail
- **Step 5:** Any financial accounts associated with both MRNs are also merged and reconciled

## 🌐 7A. MULTI-BRANCH / MULTI-TENANT ARCHITECTURE (Business Logic)

> **Think of it like this:** A hospital chain with 5 branches across the country. Each branch is like its own mini-hospital, but they all share certain things — like a franchise.

---

### Core Concept: Tenant vs. Branch

- **Multi-Tenant:** Completely separate organizations using the same software platform. Think: Dhaka Medical Group and Chittagong Healthcare — they are separate companies, their data is completely invisible to each other. They just use the same software.
- **Multi-Branch:** Same organization, multiple physical locations. Think: Square Hospital — Main Branch (Panthapath), Satellite Branch (Mirpur). They are the same company, data can be shared.

---

### Business Rules for Multi-Branch

**Patient Records:**

- A patient registered at Branch A gets an MRN that is **system-wide unique**
- If the patient visits Branch B → their full medical history from Branch A is visible (same organization = shared EMR)
- **Privacy Rule:** Sensitive records (psychiatry, HIV, reproductive health) can be flagged as "Branch Restricted" — only the treating branch can see those specific notes

**Inventory:**

- Each branch has its own inventory/store
- Medicines are tracked separately per branch
- A branch with surplus stock can initiate an **Inter-Branch Stock Transfer:**
    - Branch A requests: "Send 500 units of Amoxicillin"
    - Branch B approves and ships
    - Inventory deducted from Branch B → added to Branch A
    - Transfer document created for audit

**Financial Independence:**

- Each branch has its own Profit & Loss statement
- Corporate Finance sees consolidated reports across all branches
- Revenue from each branch flows into the same General Ledger but tagged with Branch Code for reporting

**Pricing:**

- Standard service prices can be set at corporate level (master price list)
- Branches can have a defined variance allowed (e.g., ±10% variation for local market differences)
- Branches CANNOT set prices outside the corporate-approved range without HQ Finance approval

**Reporting Hierarchy:**

- Branch Manager sees: Branch-level reports
- Regional Director sees: All branches in their region
- CFO / CEO sees: Consolidated reports across all branches and regions

## 🤖 7B. CLINICAL DECISION SUPPORT SYSTEM (CDSS)

> **Think of it like this:** An AI assistant that sits on the doctor's shoulder and whispers: "Hey, are you sure about that? This patient is allergic to this medicine!" It doesn't replace the doctor — it assists.

---

### What CDSS Checks Automatically:

1. **Drug-Allergy Interaction:** Does this medicine match any allergy in the patient's profile?
2. **Drug-Drug Interaction:** Is this new medicine dangerous when combined with medicines the patient is already taking?
3. **Drug-Disease Interaction:** Is this medicine dangerous for a patient with a specific condition? (e.g., prescribing an NSAID to a patient with kidney disease)
4. **Dosage Validation:** Is the prescribed dose within safe limits for this patient's age, weight, and kidney/liver function?
5. **Duplicate Order Alert:** Has this exact test been ordered twice for the same patient today?
6. **Diagnostic Reminders:** If patient has Diabetes (in their history) → system reminds doctor to also check HbA1c (a diabetes monitoring test) if not done in the last 3 months
7. **Critical Lab Value Alerts:** Automatically notifies the doctor if a result is outside the critical range
8. **Preventive Care Reminders:** "This patient is 55 years old and has not had a colonoscopy — consider recommending one"

### Alert Levels:

- 🟡 **Informational (Yellow):** "FYI — this combination is usually safe but monitor the patient"
- 🟠 **Warning (Orange):** "This is a significant risk — are you sure you want to proceed?" Doctor can override with a documented reason
- 🔴 **Hard Stop (Red):** "This is dangerous — prescription is BLOCKED until a second senior doctor countersigns"

## 📱 7C. TELEMEDICINE MODULE

> **Think of it like this:** The patient stays home, the doctor stays in the hospital, and they have a video call consultation — just like a Zoom meeting but with a doctor.

---

### Telemedicine Workflow:

**Booking:**

- Patient logs into the Patient Portal or App → selects "Online Consultation"
- Chooses department and doctor → sees available time slots for teleconsultation
- Books appointment → pays consultation fee via bKash/Nagad/Card online

**Consultation Day:**

- Patient receives a unique secure video link (SMS/Email)
- At the appointment time → Both patient and doctor join the video call
- Doctor has access to the patient's full EMR during the call (just like an in-person visit)
- Doctor records consultation notes in EMR during or after the call

**Prescription:**

- Doctor writes digital prescription → Patient receives it:
    - On Patient Portal (downloadable PDF)
    - Via SMS (summary)
    - Can be sent to a partner pharmacy for home delivery

**Limitations & Rules:**

- Telemedicine is only suitable for: follow-up consultations, minor ailments, prescription renewals, reports review
- Telemedicine is NOT appropriate for: first-time serious complaints requiring physical examination, emergencies, mental health crisis situations (immediate in-person care required)
- If during the teleconsult the doctor identifies something requiring in-person attention → Doctor can immediately schedule an OPD or IPD appointment

## 💳 7D. bKASH / NAGAD MOBILE PAYMENT INTEGRATION (Business Logic)

> **Think of it like this:** Instead of carrying cash or a bank card, the patient pays from their phone wallet. Like paying for a rickshaw with bKash — except here it's a hospital bill.

---

### How It Works (Business Flow):

**OPD Payment:**

- Billing officer generates the bill → total = BDT 2,500
- Patient selects "Pay via bKash"
- Billing system sends a **payment request** to the patient's registered mobile number
- Patient receives a bKash push notification on their phone → enters their bKash PIN to confirm
- bKash confirms payment to the hospital system in real-time
- System marks the bill as "Paid" → Receipt generated → Patient goes home

**Online / Patient Portal Payment:**

- Patient views their bill on the Patient Portal
- Clicks "Pay Now" → selects bKash or Nagad
- Redirected to the payment gateway → completes payment
- Portal updates in real-time → Payment confirmation shown

**IPD Advance Deposit:**

- Patient can pay advance deposit from home before coming in
- Portal allows advance payment → Amount shows in the patient's IPD account

**Reconciliation (Business-Critical):**

- Every bKash/Nagad transaction has a unique **Transaction ID**
- Hospital's Accounts Receivable team reconciles:
    - Every payment received in the bKash merchant account
    - vs. Every payment posted in the hospital billing system
- Daily reconciliation report generated automatically
- Any discrepancy (payment received in bKash but not posted in system, or vice versa) is flagged for investigation
- Transaction IDs are stored permanently in the billing system for dispute resolution

**Refunds:**

- If a refund is needed → Billing Officer initiates a refund in the system
- Refund is processed back to the patient's bKash/Nagad account
- Timeline: 3-7 business days (as per payment gateway policy)
- Refund transaction ID is recorded in the patient's account

## 📊 7E. AI ANALYTICS & BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE

> **Think of it like this:** The hospital has a very smart assistant that looks at ALL the data and tells management: "Hey, these are the trends, these are the problems, and here's what might happen next."

---

### Key Analytics Dashboards:

**For CEO / Hospital Director:**

- Total revenue today / this week / this month vs. last year
- OPD and IPD patient volumes trend
- Department-wise revenue and profitability
- Top 10 revenue-generating doctors
- Bed occupancy rate across all wards
- Average Length of Stay (ALOS) — how many days patients stay on average

**For Medical Director (Clinical Quality):**

- Readmission rates (patients re-admitted within 30 days — quality indicator)
- Infection rates (hospital-acquired infections)
- Mortality rates by department
- Surgery complication rates
- Doctor-wise patient satisfaction scores (from post-visit surveys)
- Average waiting time in OPD

**For Finance / CFO:**

- Daily cash collection
- Accounts Receivable aging (how long has money been owed?)
- Insurance claim approval vs. rejection rates (by insurance company)
- Department-wise cost analysis
- Budget vs. Actual spending

**For Pharmacy / Supply Chain:**

- Top 20 medicines by consumption
- Medicine wastage rate (expired/damaged)
- Inventory turnover ratio
- Supplier performance scores (delivery time, quality)

**Predictive Analytics:**

- "Based on seasonal trends, OPD visits will increase 30% next month — consider adding an extra doctor to the Medicine department"
- "Paracetamol stock will run out in 7 days at current consumption rate — reorder immediately"
- "Patient XYZ's vitals trend suggests risk of deterioration in the next 24 hours — alert the nurse"

**AI Fraud Detection:**

- Unusual billing patterns flagged: "Doctor A has prescribed 3x more expensive medicines this month compared to their 12-month average — review recommended"
- Unusual cash voids: "Billing Officer B voided 5 bills today — unusual pattern — audit required"

## 🏪 7F. CORPORATE CLIENT MANAGEMENT

> **Think of it like this:** A company signs a deal with the hospital — "All our 500 employees will come to your hospital. Give us a discount." This is a corporate account.

---

### Corporate Account Business Logic:

**Setup:**

- A corporate contract is created:
    - Company name, authorized contact
    - List of covered employees (can be uploaded as a list)
    - Services covered (OPD only? OPD + IPD? Specific departments?)
    - Discount structure (e.g., 15% on OPD consultation, 10% on medicines, room rate BDT 5,000/night)
    - Credit limit (e.g., Company X gets up to BDT 5,00,000 on credit — billed monthly)
    - Contract validity period

**Service Delivery:**

- Employee comes to hospital → shows company ID card or staff ID
- Front Desk searches the corporate account → verifies employee is on the active list
- Services rendered → charges accumulated against the corporate account (not personal billing)
- Employee pays their share (if any co-pay is in the contract)

**Invoicing:**

- At end of each month → system generates a consolidated invoice for the corporate client
    - Itemized: every employee, every visit, every service
- Invoice sent to company's finance/HR department
- Company pays via bank transfer (B2B payment)

**Credit Control:**

- If corporate client's outstanding balance exceeds the credit limit → system alerts billing
- New services may be blocked (or put on cash-only) until balance is reduced

## 🎟️ 7G. SUBSCRIPTION / HEALTH PACKAGE LOGIC

> **Think of it like this:** Like a Netflix subscription but for healthcare. Pay a fixed amount, get defined services throughout the year.

---

### Package Types:

**OPD Package:**

- Example: "Annual Wellness Package — BDT 5,000/year"
- Includes: 12 OPD consultations (any doctor), 2 complete blood tests, 1 health checkup

**Family Package:**

- Covers 4 family members under one subscription

**Disease Management Package:**

- Example: "Diabetes Management Package — BDT 8,000/year"
- Includes: 12 endocrinologist consultations, quarterly HbA1c tests, eye exam, foot exam

### Business Logic:

- Package benefits are tracked per subscriber:
    - How many OPD visits used vs. remaining?
    - How many lab tests used vs. remaining?
- When a patient with an active package comes in:
    - System automatically applies the package benefit
    - Deducts from the remaining balance
    - If package benefit is exhausted → patient is informed → charged normal rates for additional services
- Package expiry: If the year ends and some benefits are unused → most packages are "use it or lose it" (unused benefits expire — this must be clearly communicated at purchase)
- Auto-renewal reminder sent 30 days before expiry via SMS/Email

## 🔐 7H. AUDIT TRAIL & COMPLIANCE

> **Think of it like this:** Every single action in the system is recorded — WHO did WHAT to WHICH record at WHAT TIME. Like a security camera, but for data.

---

### What is Tracked in the Audit Trail:

- Every login and logout (with IP address and device)
- Every patient record viewed (even if no changes made — for privacy compliance)
- Every change to a prescription (original value vs. new value)
- Every billing modification (who changed what amount, when)
- Every discount or waiver approved (who authorized it)
- Every deletion attempt (deletions are almost never truly deleted — records are "deactivated" and preserved)
- Every role/permission change (who gave who access to what)
- Every failed login attempt (security monitoring)

### Compliance Standards Supported:

- **HIPAA-equivalent** patient data privacy rules
- **Bangladesh DGDA** (drug regulatory authority) compliance for pharmacy
- **Health Ministry** reporting requirements
- **Tax authority** financial reporting standards
- **ISO 9001 / JCI Accreditation** quality standards

### Data Retention Policy:

- Patient medical records: Kept for minimum **10 years** after last visit (legal requirement)
- Financial records: Kept for minimum **7 years** (tax law)
- Audit logs: Kept for minimum **5 years**
- After retention period → data archived (not deleted — moved to cold storage)
